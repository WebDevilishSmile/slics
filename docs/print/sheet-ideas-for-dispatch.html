ON CALL SHEET: ideas for an easier sheet

Suggestions for dispatch from the SLICs app · November 16, 2026

Dispatch keeps the ON CALL SHEET going every week, often in a hurry and, on bid day, with the phone ringing. We've read every weekly tab since May and every edit since October, and a few ideas kept coming up that might make the sheet quicker to fill in, easier for a new dispatcher to pick up, and harder to get wrong. They're only suggestions. Take any, all or none, in whatever order suits you. None of them changes how the bid works, and the sheet stays yours.

Every name in this document is made up. Where we mention something we saw in the sheet, it's to show why an idea might help, not to point at anyone. The companion sheet, How the app reads the weekly cover tabs, describes the sheet as it's kept today; nothing here undoes it.

If you only try three

  1. Freeze the header rows (8). One minute, and every tab is easier to scroll.
  2. Link can view; dispatch can edit (13). Five minutes, and the sheet is much harder to wipe by mistake.
  3. A Notes column (2). Notes get a home that drivers and the app both read correctly.

What stays the same

The tab named by its Saturday's date, the title row, the two header rows, names as LAST, FIRST, the pick numbers, and how the bid runs. Each idea here is a small addition or a tidy-up around those.

1Shifter jobs in a place of their own

Today the shifter jobs sit at the top of the job list in gray, and each row is hidden so drivers don't see them as jobs to bid on. It works, but it's easy to miss a row, and a hidden row is easy to forget is there.

Seen: on W/E 11/28, seven shifter rows hidden at the top, and one of them (SH07) listed again further down among the bid jobs, in orange and not hidden.

Option A Works today A tab of their own each week, like SHIFTERS 11/28/2026. The app only reads tabs named with the date alone, so drivers never see it, and nothing needs hiding.

Option B After an app update On the same tab, under a heading row SHIFTER JOBS (NOT FOR BID) below the bid jobs. If you'd prefer this, we'll teach the app to stop at that heading first and let you know when it's safe.

2A Notes column

Add a column headed Notes to the right of Description. The app doesn't read it, so anything typed there stays a note: "STA needed", "3rd move from LV81", "no route".

Seen: STA NEEDED typed under NAME, which the app reads as a driver by that name taking the job; three "3RD MOVE FROM …" rows with no Job #; NO ROUTE under NAME.

  1. Right-click the Description column letter → Insert 1 column right.
  2. Type Notes in the header row of that column.

3Headings in the pick list

A row with *COVER DRIVERS 1-20 under NAME before driver 1, and *ON CALL 21 AND UP before driver 21. The asterisk tells the app it's a heading, not a driver, so it skips the row. A new dispatcher sees where the cover list ends without counting.

Worth knowing: in the pick list, unlike the job list, a hidden row is still read. A hidden name with a call-in time shows in the app as a driver without a number. Deleting the row keeps it out (last week's tab still has it).

4Sleeper teams in their own section

After an app update The ER seats under a SLEEPER TEAMS heading at the end of the job list: the seat, who's covering it, whose seat it is, and why. The covering driver's own row then says COVERING ER18013, as it does today.

Seen: the seats already gather at the end of the list, next to the "3rd move" notes. A heading would make that official. Drivers never see sleeper seats in the app either way.

Why a word beats a color, every time

The app reads the words in cells and nothing else: no colors, no hidden rows in the pick list, no comments. A heading, a note or a status in a cell reads the same to a new dispatcher, a driver on their phone, and the app.

2 · Words, colors and a template

5Dropdowns for the common words

A dropdown on Cover Reason and on the pick list's job / status cell lets dispatch pick a word instead of typing it, and every week reads the same. With "Show a warning", anything else can still be typed, so a job code or a one-off still goes in.

Seen: on one tab, PEAK, PEAK JOB and peak for the same reason; RETIRED and EXTRA CPU beside VACATION. In 16 weeks, nearly 50 wordings for about a dozen reasons.

A starting list, to change as dispatch sees fit:

Cover ReasonJob / status
VACATION · FMLA · VACANT · PEAK JOB · NEW JOB · EXTRA JOB · COMBO WEEK · VOLUNTARY LAYOFF · REDUCTION OF WORKFORCE · P/H · RETIRED · OTHERON CALL · VACATION · FMLA · BUYOUT · PASS · NO ANSWER · DIS · LIGHT DUTY · WORKERS COMP · VOLUNTARY LAYOFF · TWI AND NIGHT (MON-THU)
  1. Select the Cover Reason column (click its letter).
  2. Data → Data validation → Add rule.
  3. Criteria: Dropdown. Type each word on its own line.
  4. Under Advanced options: Show a warning. Done.

Once it's on the template (9), every new week has it.

6One code per job

Each job with a code of its own, in capitals and without spaces, for the whole week. Drivers' lists and the app's history follow the code, so two jobs sharing one get mixed up.

Seen: on W/E 11/28, XTR99 on two rows with different start times, CARRIER SHIFT used as the code for two jobs, and xtr88 in lower case. Something like CS1 and CS2 keeps them apart.

7Colors with a key, or a word

Colors carry a lot of meaning in the sheet today: gray rows, a yellow Job # on every job, two shades of yellow behind names in the pick list, red and light-blue fills on hidden rows, an orange row. Someone new can't tell what each one means, and the app can't see them at all.

Two easy fixes, either or both: a key, a short row at the top right of the tab saying what each color means, or the meaning in a word (the Notes column, a heading, a status) beside the color.

8Freeze the header rows

With the title and the job header frozen, the column names stay at the top while you scroll down a 150-row tab.

  1. Open the week's tab.
  2. View → Freeze → 2 rows.

Seen: no tab in the file has frozen rows today.

9A clean template for each new week

Most weeks start as a copy of last week's tab, which brings along last week's hidden rows, notes, colors and title. A TEMPLATE tab that's blank but ready (title, both header rows, the Notes column, the pick-list headings, the dropdowns, frozen rows) gives every week the same clean start.

Seen: since May, 2 tabs had no title row and 2 kept the title of another week, so the app couldn't save those weeks. The light shading on days a job doesn't run stops at a fixed row on each tab, so jobs added below it don't get it.

  1. Make the TEMPLATE tab once: copy a week, clear the jobs and names, type JOBS AVAILABLE FOR W/E in the title.
  2. Protect it with a warning: Data → Protect sheets and ranges → Add a sheet or range → Sheet → Set permissions → Show a warning when editing this range.
  3. Each new week: right-click the TEMPLATE tab → Duplicate, rename it to the Saturday's date, and add the date to the title.

The file already has a protected Sample On Call tab, so the idea isn't new to it.

Building a week in private

Building next week under a working name, like DRAFT 11/28, and renaming it to the date when it's ready means drivers get one "Cover jobs posted" for the finished week instead of following it as it's typed in. It pairs well with the template.

3 · The file, and keeping it safe

10Archive the old tabs

The file has 530 tabs going back to March 2022: 247 weekly job lists, 202 ON CALL tabs, 34 hub driver lists, 17 "Copy of" tabs, 13 interest lists and 9 tabs still named "Sheet…". The Jobs tab is the very last one. That's a lot of scrolling to reach this week, and a big file is slower to open on a phone.

One way that keeps everything: make a copy of the whole file as an archive, then remove the old tabs from the working file, keeping the last two months or so.

  1. File → Make a copy, named ON CALL SHEET ARCHIVE 2022-2026.
  2. Open the copy and check it has everything.
  3. Back in the working file, right-click each old tab → Delete. (Or, more gently, Hide sheet.)

The app only reads the current and coming weeks, and it keeps its own copy of past weeks, so an archive doesn't change anything drivers see.

11Tabs in order, one naming style, colors

The tabs everyone uses every week first (Jobs, SHIFTERS, the driver lists), then the weeks, newest first. One name style for each kind (11/28/2026, ON CALL 11/28/2026), and one tab color per kind: right-click a tab → Change color.

Seen: dates written at least six ways in tab names (11/28/2026, 01/10/2026, 8/30/25, 3.26.2022, on call 02.04.23, oncall12.10.22), and the week of 1/17/2026 named 1/17/2025.

12A look at the ON CALL tab's dates

On ON CALL 11/14/2026, the Date: above each day shows 12/25/1899, 12/26/1899 and so on. That's what Google Sheets shows when a date is worked out from an empty cell. The weeks before it show the right dates, so the cell the days count from was probably left blank when the tab was made. Typing the week's first date there fixes all five days.

13Link can view; dispatch can edit

Today anyone with the link can edit the sheet, and a link travels easily. One wrong paste or an accidental delete from anyone who has it, and a week is gone. Making the link view-only and adding each dispatcher as an editor (each needs a Google account) keeps everyone who edits today able to, and everyone else able to look.

  1. Share → add each dispatcher's email as Editor.
  2. Under General access: Anyone with the link → Viewer.
  3. The gear at the top → untick Editors can change permissions and share.

The SLICs app needs the link to stay open for viewing, which this keeps. It's the sheet owner's call, and usually the owner's change to make.

14Phone numbers in a private file

The SHIFTERS tab lists drivers' personal phone numbers, and the ON CALL tabs hold notes from calls. Anyone with the link can open the whole file, every tab included. A separate small file for phone numbers, shared only with dispatch, keeps them close at hand without being on a link that's been passed around.

15Name a version when a week posts

Google keeps every change, but finding "how it looked when we posted" is hard in a long list. Naming the moment takes a few seconds and makes it easy to look back, or put a week back as it was.

  1. File → Version history → Name current version.
  2. Type something like W/E 11/28 posted.
